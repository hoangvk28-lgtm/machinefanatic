# Dust collectors and cyclone separators
- Single-stage: debris passes through the impeller into a bag or cartridge; cheap, but fine dust loads the filter and suction falls.
- Two-stage/cyclone: chips drop into a bin before air reaches the filter; filter stays clean far longer, and can use a finer filter.
- Quoted CFM is meaningful only at a stated static pressure; a 2 HP unit can drop from 1,400 CFM at 2 in. to 1,000 CFM at 6.5 in.
- Static pressure is resistance from hose, bends and reductions; flex hose loses far more than smooth duct.
- 1 micron felt bag or pleated cartridge is the standard filter upgrade for single-stage units.
- Home shop with 3+ tools: cyclone around 1,200+ CFM with 6 in. main duct is the usual advice; small units suit one tool at a time with 4 in. hose.
- Fine dust (under 10 microns) is the health concern; bags rated 5 micron pass the finest dust back into the room.
- Wall-mount units save floor space but need studs and the bag hangs at head height.
- Retrofit cyclone separators sit between a shop vacuum and the tool, protecting the vacuum filter; hose diameter matters (2.5 in. vs 4 in.).
- Static buildup in plastic ducting and separators is a nuisance; grounding and anti-static vanes help.
- Ambient air filters clean airborne dust but do not capture at the tool.
- Empty bags before they are over half full; suction drops quickly with a full bag.
- Induction motors run quieter and last longer than brushed universal motors.
- Dust is explosive in rare conditions; keep collectors away from grinders and sparks.
# Planers
- Straight-knife heads use 2 to 4 disposable knives; helical heads use many small carbide inserts that rotate to a fresh edge.
- Helical heads are quieter and leave less tear-out; straight-knife planers cost roughly 30 percent less.
- Snipe depends mainly on bed and table alignment and board support, not cutterhead type.
- Benchtop planers use double-edge disposable knives; stationary planers use resharpenable HSS knives.
- Take shallow passes (1/32 to 1/16 in.); feed rate in feet per minute affects finish.
- Dust port output matters; planers make huge chip volumes and need a collector.
- Hand planers: 3-1/4 in. width standard, adjustable depth to about 1/8 in.; cordless units trade runtime for freedom of cord.
# Tower fans and evaporative coolers
- CFM is the headline airflow figure; oscillation angle governs coverage (none to 90 degrees seen).
- Night mode and multiple speeds matter for bedroom noise.
- Bladeless units are usually pricier and can move less air, apart from top brands; they are safer around kids and pets.
- Evaporative coolers add moisture and work poorly in humid climates; need water tank refills and pad care.
- Tower fans suit narrow spaces; tall form factor tips more easily.
- Remote control and timers are common features.
# Water softeners
- Ion exchange swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium; rated capacity is grains removed before regeneration.
- Hardness measured in grains per gallon; size capacity from daily gallons times hardness.
- 16,000 to 32,000 grain units suit most homes; large households may need up to 100,000.
- Salt-free (template assisted crystallization) conditions scale but does not remove hardness; a conditioner, not a softener.
- Sources disagree on salt-free thresholds; confirm hardness with a lab test.
- Salt-based systems need salt refills and discharge brine wastewater.
- Resin lasts roughly 10 to 15 years; iron in water fouls resin.
