# Floor jacks research notes

- A floor jack is a lifting tool only; support the vehicle on rated jack stands before going under it. Never rely on hydraulics alone.
- Rated capacity is for the jack at full extension on a hard level surface; a 3 ton jack lifts a corner or end, not the whole vehicle weight, so tonnage should exceed half the gross vehicle weight with margin.
- Minimum saddle height decides whether the jack fits under a lowered car; 3 to 4 inches is low profile, 5 inches plus fits most stock cars and SUVs.
- Maximum lift height should clear the lift point plus stand height; trucks and SUVs often need 18 inches or more.
- Dual-piston quick-lift pumps raise the saddle in fewer strokes until it contacts the load, then switch to a slower high-force stage.
- Steel jacks weigh 60 to 80 lb; aluminum or hybrid racing jacks run roughly 30 to 45 lb, easier to move but costlier and dent-prone.
- Overload relief valves guard against pumping past capacity; bypass valves stop at full extension.
- Handle length gives leverage and clearance; telescoping or two-piece handles ease storage.
- Swivel casters and a long chassis improve positioning; a long reach helps get under bumpers and rocker panels.
- Saddle pads (rubber or cross-shaped) protect pinch welds and frame rails; extra saddles help for trucks.
- Release the load slowly by turning the handle counterclockwise a fraction; a fast release drops the vehicle.
- Hydraulic oil leaks and air in the system cause creeping or soft lifts; bleed by opening the release valve and cycling the pump with the handle in the socket.
- Store upright and fully lowered, and top up hydraulic jack oil (not brake fluid or motor oil) with the ram retracted.
- Cheap jacks vary in castings and welds; look for third-party standards such as ASME PASE or independent test listings, and be skeptical of unlabeled capacity claims.
- 2 ton jacks suit compact cars; 3 ton is the common all-rounder for SUVs and light trucks; 4 to 5 ton is for heavier trucks and shop use.
- House or basement floor jacks (adjustable posts) are structural screw posts, not automotive jacks.
