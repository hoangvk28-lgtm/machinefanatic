# Inverter generators research notes

- Size by rated (running) watts, not the peak or surge number on the box; peak only covers a few seconds of motor startup.
- Peak figure is often 20 to 30 percent above rated; a "2500W" label may mean about 2000 rated.
- Motor loads (fridge, pump, AC compressor) can need 2 to 3 times running watts at start.
- Inverter design converts engine AC to DC then to clean AC, so engine speed can follow load; this saves fuel and cuts noise.
- Low THD (under about 3 percent) matters for laptops, phones, CPAP and modern appliance boards.
- Eco or economy mode lets the engine idle down at light load; leave it off for sudden large loads.
- Propane or dual fuel typically gives lower output than gasoline (often 10 percent or more less) but stores indefinitely.
- Automatic CO shutdown is a worthwhile feature; never run any generator indoors, in a garage or near windows; keep it well away from the house.
- Parallel-ready models let two small units combine for more output via a parallel kit.
- Noise ratings are measured at a set distance and part load; real noise at full load is higher.
- Tank size and runtime at 25 percent load differ a lot; check the listing runtime basis.
- Fresh gasoline with stabilizer, or running the carburetor dry before storage, prevents gummed jets, the top failure cause.
- Change oil after the first 20 hours or so, then per the manual; small crankcases run hot and hold little oil.
- Low-oil shutdown protects the engine but stops it mid-job if oil is low.
- Open-frame generators are cheaper per watt but louder and dirtier power than inverters.
- Long full-load running shortens life; size so typical load is around 50 to 70 percent.
- Spark arrestor and altitude: output drops about 3 percent per 1,000 ft of elevation.
- Run it monthly under load or drain the fuel; ethanol blends absorb water.
