# Nailers research notes (framing, finish, pin; brad guides exist separately)

- Gauge: lower number is thicker. Framing nails run 0.113 to 0.131 in shank; 15 and 16 gauge finish nails; 18 gauge brads; 23 gauge headless pins.
- Framing nailers: 21 degree plastic-collated full round head, 28 degree clipped or offset head, 30 to 34 degree paper-collated clipped heads, and 15 degree wire-coil roofing/siding. Magazine angle must match the nails exactly.
- Some jurisdictions require full round head nails for structural framing; clipped or offset heads may not be allowed by local code. Check before buying.
- Framing nailers weigh roughly 7 to 9 lb and use 80 to 120 PSI; cordless gas-free versions are heavier at 9 to 11 lb and slower to cycle.
- Sequential trigger (one nail per trigger pull, nose touched first) is safer; bump or contact firing is fast for sheathing but injures when misused. Selectable triggers cover both.
- Depth adjustment (tool-free) and a nose that can be reversed or fitted with a nose cover affect finish quality and marring.
- Finish nailers: 15 gauge angled (up to about 2.5 in, for baseboards, crown, door casings) and 16 gauge straight (lighter, smaller holes). 15 gauge holds heavier trim better.
- Pin nailers (23 gauge) have almost no holding power; they pin small moldings and glued joints; use glue and clamp. They are not trim nailers.
- Pneumatic nailers need a compressor delivering enough SCFM at 90 PSI; a small pancake compressor handles trim guns but struggles with a framing nailer used rapidly.
- Cordless nailers (battery) avoid hoses and compressors; frame-class battery framing nailers cost more and add weight, finish and pin sizes are practical.
- Oil-free air tools avoid oil stains; others need a few drops of air tool oil daily.
- Jams: use correct nail length and collation; most modern guns have tool-free jam release and nosepiece latches.
- Dry-fire lockout prevents the nailer firing when nails run low and marring workpieces.
- Safety: wear eye and hearing protection, never point at anyone, disconnect air before clearing jams or leaving the tool.
- Compressed air line: use a 3/8 in hose with 1/4 in NPT fittings; a smaller hose starves a framing gun.
- Nail length is limited by the tool; confirm the nails fit the magazine, and mind the head diameter for 21 degree nails.
