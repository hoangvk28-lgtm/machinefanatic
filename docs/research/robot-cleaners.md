# Robot vacuums, vacuum mops (floor washers), robotic pool cleaners, window cleaning robots
Written 2026-11-14 from web searches (Narwal, Best Buy, Digit and Make Tech Easier robot vacuum guides; iGarden, Beatbot, Maytronics and PoolPartsToGo pool robot guides; GadgetGuy, CyberShack and Which? window robot guides; Tom's Guide and Dreame wet-dry vacuum guides). Most sources are vendor or affiliate guides: state findings as general buying knowledge, never quote vendor numbers as tested facts.

## Robot vacuums
- Navigation: LiDAR maps work in complete darkness and give efficient, consistent paths; camera-only navigation is less consistent in complex layouts and in low light. Gyroscope/random-bounce models are the budget tier.
- Obstacle avoidance is where cameras (or structured light / AI front sensors) earn their place: cords, socks, toys, pet waste. A camera matters most for homes with kids, pets or clutter. A cheaper robot with good avoidance can beat a camera model that handles clutter badly.
- Privacy: LiDAR-only robots without a camera avoid having a camera moving through the home. "Without apps" models run from a remote or buttons; fewer features (no maps, no no-go zones), no account or cloud.
- Suction: Pa numbers rise every year; the difference between 6,000 and 8,000 Pa is small in daily use. Brushroll design (rubber, anti-tangle) matters more for hair.
- Carpet: check pile height and threshold climbing (often about 2 cm on mid-range units). High pile is hard for most robots; mop pads must lift or detach on carpet.
- Self-emptying docks: bag-based docks need replacement bags; the bag or bin still needs emptying roughly every 30 to 60 days, and the dock filter needs periodic replacement. Check parts availability. Mop-washing docks need clean-water refills, dirty-water emptying and occasional descaling.
- Subscriptions: some brands tie features or consumables to accounts; a "no subscription" pick works fully without paid plans.
- Multiple floors: multi-map storage (often 3 to 5 maps); the robot must be carried between floors.
- Low profile: check height against furniture clearance; LiDAR turrets add height.

## Vacuum mops / wet-dry floor washers
- Wet mode only on sealed floors; not on unsealed or waxed wood or unfinished stone, where water can seep in.
- Two-tank design keeps dirty water out of the mopping path, unlike a string mop. Better units use little water and recover it in the same pass; adjustable water flow helps on wood and laminate.
- Self-cleaning cycles flush the roller and tube; still empty and rinse the dirty tank after each use to stop odor, and dry the roller.
- Scratch risk comes from trapped grit: keep the roller clean and pick up large debris first.
- Edge reach: a head that lies flat and cleans to the wall matters for kitchens.
- Running costs: rollers, filters, cleaning solution; some mops use disposable pads (ongoing cost and waste). Steam mops are a different tool: heat, no dirty-water recovery; avoid on unsealed wood and some laminate (check the maker's floor guidance).
- Weight: floor washers with full tanks are heavy (often 10 to 13 lb); matters for seniors.

## Robotic pool cleaners
- Corded: no battery to wear out; can run as long as needed; the cable (and tangling, swivel) is the limit. Better for large or heavily used pools and daily runs.
- Cordless: easy, no cable; runtime is the battery limit for the mode you use, not the headline figure; lithium battery ages; must be retrieved (some surface automatically).
- Pool type: an above-ground-only model in an in-ground pool cannot climb walls. Check liner compatibility for vinyl.
- Filters: many residential models use about 180-micron filters (sand, pollen, most insects); finer filters catch fine dust but clog faster with leaves; dual-layer baskets combine both. Large leaf loads need a wide intake and big basket.
- Waterline cleaning targets the scum ring; floor-only models skip walls and waterline. Steps are often missed by robots.
- Size: small pools under about 650 sq ft suit almost any decent robot; check the rated pool size and cable length (cable should reach the far end of the pool plus depth).
- Robots run independently of the pool pump and filter (no booster pump, unlike pressure-side cleaners).
- Ownership: rinse the filter basket after each run, store out of direct sun, keep a caddy for heavy units (lifting a wet robot is a real issue for seniors).

## Window cleaning robots
- Most are corded (AC via extension cord); a backup battery (UPS) holds the robot on the glass for about 20 to 30 minutes during a power cut and sounds an alarm.
- Safety rope/tether anchored to a fixed point indoors is the last backstop; always use it, especially on exterior or high-rise glass.
- Suction listings run roughly 2,800 to 6,400 Pa; battery and rope matter more for safety than a higher number.
- Glass must be dry: no rain, snow or high humidity; avoid windy or very hot days.
- Frameless glass: edge detection claims are marketing; no reliable independent evidence on frameless panes. Say so honestly.
- Square vs round pads; water-spray models wet the pad automatically (better on grime than dry pads with hand spraying).
- High-rise exterior use: rope, UPS and wind are the deciding factors; check local building rules.
