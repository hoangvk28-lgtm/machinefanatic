# Pumps research (sump, utility, transfer, sewage, well, pool, heat pump water heater)

## Sump pumps
- Rated GPH is measured at zero lift; real output falls as head rises. Sources cite roughly 10% loss per 5 ft of lift, or 30-50% loss at 10 ft; the pump curve is the number to trust.
- Horsepower is a weak proxy: two pumps with the same HP can move very different volumes at the same height.
- Sizing: time the pit fill rate (gallons per minute) x 60 = GPH inflow; add a 1.5x to 2x margin for heavy rain.
- Total dynamic head = static vertical lift + pipe friction loss (elbows, long runs, small pipe). Pick the pump that meets target flow at that head on its curve.
- Most homes are served by a 1/3 HP primary pump; typical flow around 2,000 to 2,700 GPH at a 10 ft lift.
- Backup pumps usually move about 1/3 to 1/2 of the primary's volume; they are insurance, not a second primary.
- Battery backup runtime depends on amp-hours and how often the pump cycles; roughly 5 to 7 hours continuous pumping on a typical lead-acid setup, longer on larger or LiFePO4 banks if cycling is light.
- Battery failure modes: aged battery that will not hold load, corroded terminals, skipped maintenance and testing. Modified sine inverters can cause motor hum and overheating.
- Water-powered backups need pressurized municipal water; useless on a well or cistern, and they use about 1 gallon of city water per gallon of sump water moved.
- Neither backup helps against a stuck float, failed check valve or failed motor; alarms and testing matter.
- Float switch is the most common failure point: stuck, tangled on cord or discharge pipe, or jammed against pit wall. Many "dead" pumps are stuck floats, jammed check valves or frozen discharge lines.
- Failed check valve shows as water falling back into the pit after shutoff, causing short cycling and wear.
- Pedestal motors sit dry in open air, can last long and are cheaper to service but are louder and need a wider pit; submersibles are quieter and easier to seal under a lid (radon systems).
- Lifespan depends more on cycling frequency than on type. Cast iron vs thermoplastic lifespan claims are unverified; state only that cast iron sheds heat better and thermoplastic is lighter and corrosion proof.
- Test by pouring water into the pit a few times a year; clean the inlet screen; keep the pit free of gravel and debris.

## Utility, transfer and sewage pumps (engineering basics, well established)
- Utility pumps are for draining flooded floors, pools and tubs; many go down to about 1/8 to 1/4 inch of water; they are not meant for continuous sump duty.
- Sewage pumps pass solids (typically 2 inch spherical solids) via vortex or semi-open impellers; effluent pumps pass about 3/4 inch solids. Do not use a sump pump on sewage.
- Transfer pumps move clean water from tank to tank, rain barrels, boats; check hose size and self-priming ability.

## Well, pool pumps (engineering basics)
- Shallow well jet pumps lift from about 25 ft or less; deep wells need submersible or deep-well jet. Jet pumps need priming and a pressure switch and tank.
- Pool pumps: variable speed pumps cut energy sharply versus single speed (pump affinity laws: halving speed cuts power use to about one eighth). Match flow to filter and plumbing size.
- Pool cover pumps are small submersible units that drain rainwater off a cover; they need to run down to about 1/8 inch.

## Heat pump water heaters
- Federal labels use UEF for efficiency and first hour rating (FHR) for delivery capacity; HPWHs run UEF about 3.5 to 4.5 versus roughly 0.92 for a standard electric tank.
- FHR must match peak-hour demand; rough tank guide 50 gal for 1 to 3 people, 65 gal for 3 to 4, 80 gal for 4 to 6.
- Heat-pump-only mode recovers slower than resistance elements; sizing up one tier is common. Hybrid mode adds elements.
- They pull heat from room air and cool the space; needs enough air volume or ducting and a condensate drain; check the manual for operating air temperature range.
- Noise is similar to a dehumidifier or small fridge, roughly 45 to 55 dB; check the spec sheet.
- Clearance requirements vary by model; verify in the installation manual. Tax credit rules change, check current rules.
