# Generator transfer switches research notes

- A transfer switch isolates the house from the utility while a generator feeds selected circuits, preventing backfeed onto utility lines, which endangers line workers and breaks code.
- Manual transfer switches (MTS) power only the circuits wired into the switch box, typically 4 to 12; an interlock kit on the main panel can power any breaker but needs a listed kit for the panel.
- Automatic transfer switches (ATS) detect an outage and start/transfer on their own; they pair with standby generators and cost more and need professional install.
- Match the switch to the generator receptacle: a 30A L14-30 inlet suits most portables (about 3,600W at 120V, 7,200W at 240V); 50A suits larger units.
- Never connect a 50A generator outlet to a 30A inlet and wiring; overload can occur without tripping the generator breaker.
- Wire from a 30A inlet box to the switch is generally 10 gauge or larger; follow the manual and local code.
- Inlet box should be outdoor rated (NEMA 3R minimum); power cord must match the inlet.
- Small 15A and 20A pre-wired switches serve a single circuit (furnace, fridge, sump pump) and use a 5-15P or 5-20P plug; at 120V this is about 1,800W and 2,400W.
- 125V single-pole switches do 120V only; 120/240V switches have two poles and handle both legs of a split-phase panel.
- Amp rating must exceed the largest load; 100A to 200A switches are service-level units tied to the main panel and typically need an electrician.
- A permit and inspection is required in many jurisdictions for inlet boxes and transfer switches; many places require a licensed electrician.
- Look for UL or ETL listing; unlisted switches may fail inspection or insurance requirements.
- Buy the generator first, then size the switch to its output and receptacle type.
- Whole-home generators (for example 22kW class) ship with their own ATS sized to the service, commonly 200A.
- Dual-power ATS modules (DIN rail, 63A to 160A) from generic sellers are often rated for 220V single-phase or 50/60Hz and are not substitutes for a UL-listed residential ATS.
- Ground and neutral handling: do not bond neutral twice; follow the manual on switched versus unswitched neutral.
- Label the panel and keep a written load list; start big motor loads one at a time.
