# Wet/dry (shop) vacuums research notes

- Peak HP on the box is the motor's locked-rotor or no-load surge figure, not sustained output; it cannot be compared across brands. Real suction is airflow (CFM) plus sealed lift (inches of water lift).
- Airflow moves debris, sealed suction lifts it; wide hoses (2.5 in) favor CFM and heavy debris, narrow hoses (1.25 in) favor suction for fine dust.
- Tank gallons are a nominal size; usable volume is lower, and wet pickup should stay under roughly 80 percent full.
- Wet pickup needs the paper or cloth dry filter removed and a foam sleeve fitted; pulling wet debris through a paper filter ruins it.
- A float shutoff closes the intake when the tank fills so water never reaches the motor; confirm the model has one.
- Fine drywall, concrete and sanding dust clogs a standard cartridge fast and passes through bags; use a fine dust bag or a HEPA-rated filter plus a cyclone or pre-separator.
- HEPA claims need a sealed system (gaskets, sealed filter seat); a HEPA cartridge in a leaky tank still exhausts fine dust. Silica dust work calls for a true dust extractor.
- Blower ports (exhaust port) clear leaves, sawdust and corners; the exhaust is dirty and the filter should be removed or an exhaust hose used carefully.
- Corded units run indefinitely; cordless shop vacs run roughly 10 to 25 minutes on a battery and have smaller tanks, which suits cars and quick cleanups.
- Battery platform matters: tool-only versions leverage existing 18V/20V packs; a tool-only price can hide the cost of a battery and charger.
- Plastic tanks resist dents and are quieter; stainless tanks resist rust and wet-chemical use but add weight and noise.
- Casters: four swivel wheels or a large rear wheel pair affect stability and stair carrying; tip-overs are common on tall small-footprint tanks.
- Cord length (12 to 20 ft) and hose length (7 to 20 ft) govern reach; a short cord forces extension cords with proper gauge.
- Noise: standard shop vacs run about 85 to 95 dB; quiet designs aim lower but cost more.
- Replace cartridge filters when airflow drops after cleaning; wash foam sleeves, empty the tank after wet use and leave the lid open to dry to prevent mildew and corrosion.
- Vacuum-mop floor washers (Tineco, Bissell CrossWave, roborock) are a separate home-floor category: clean-water and dirty-water tanks, brush roll, 20 to 50 minute runtime; they do not handle gravel or large debris.
- Self-cleaning cycles on floor washers flush the roller and tubes; schedule filter, roller and tank rinsing weekly.
- Never vacuum flammable liquids, hot embers or ash unless the machine is rated for it; a standard motor sparks.
