CORRECTION 1, THE IMAGE MANIFEST. This is the part of batch 1 I had to redo entirely.

In batch 1 you returned 51 filenames that pointed at only 18 source photos. One photo appeared under eight different filenames, each with alt text describing a scene that photo does not contain. A regulator close up was captioned as a generator connected to a propane cylinder. A refrigerator compressor was captioned as a sump pump. A photo of generators on a shop shelf, with retail price tags legible in the frame, was captioned as a generator placed safely away from a house. None of that was usable.

So, for this batch:

- One filename equals one distinct source photo. Never rename the same photo twice. If you have ten figure slots, you need ten different photographs.
- Write the alt text and the caption from what is actually in the frame, not from what the section needs. Open the photo and describe it. If the only true description of a photo does not fit the section, that is the wrong photo, so find another or leave the slot out.
- Reject any photo with a price tag, a price sticker or a shelf label visible anywhere in the frame. The site never displays prices.
- Reject any photo with text, arrows or labels drawn on top of it. That is a diagram, not a photograph.
- Reject any photo that is a product listing shot on a white background, watermarked, or carrying a retailer logo.
- A photo whose subject sits in a laboratory, a factory or a shop display is usually the wrong photo for a consumer article even when the hardware is technically correct. Prefer a real home, yard, campsite, van or workshop.
- If you cannot find four honest photographs for an article, deliver three and say which slot you could not fill and what you searched. Three true photos beat five captioned wrongly.

Useful note from batch 1: Wikimedia Commons category trees were far more productive than keyword search. Category:Electric generators, Category:Uninterruptible power supplies, Category:Oscilloscopes, Category:Electrical wiring and Category:Propane all contained usable photographs that keyword search never surfaced.
