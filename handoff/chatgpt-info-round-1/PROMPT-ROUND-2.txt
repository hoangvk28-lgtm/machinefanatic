HOW TO KEEP TWENTY ARTICLES FROM OVERLAPPING

This cluster has more internal overlap than the generator one. Panel ratings, wiring, controllers, batteries and system sizing all touch each other, so decide the boundaries before you write and link across instead of repeating.

- Articles 1, 3, 7, 12 and 15 are about the panel itself: cell type, rated versus real output, flexible versus rigid construction, bifacial construction, degradation and warranty terms. Keep system design out of them.
- Articles 2, 9 and 20 are about sizing a system. Article 2 is the general method, article 9 applies it to an RV, article 20 applies it to boondocking with a real daily load budget. They must not run the same worksheet three times, so each takes a different angle on the arithmetic.
- Articles 4, 5 and 16 are about getting energy from the panel into the battery: controller type, controller sizing, and the physical wiring. Keep panel specs out of them.
- Articles 6 and 11 are about array configuration and placement: series versus parallel, angle and shading.
- Articles 13 and 14 are about the battery: depth of discharge, and chemistry comparison.
- Articles 17, 18 and 19 are RV systems that consume the power rather than produce it. Each should end by pointing at the sizing articles rather than sizing anything itself.
- Article 8 is about portable and folding panels specifically, so it owns everything about deployment, stands, and taking panels in and out of a vehicle.

RESEARCH NOTES FOR THIS CLUSTER

Three things competitors in this niche consistently get wrong or skip. Verify each before using it, and if a source does not support it, leave it out.

- Panel ratings are measured at standard test conditions, which include a cell temperature that real panels in sunlight exceed. Most pages quote the rated watt figure without explaining the test condition behind it or the temperature coefficient printed on the same label.
- Charge controller sizing is usually presented as a single formula, when the limits that actually bite are the controller's maximum input voltage against the array's open circuit voltage in cold weather, and its output current against the battery bank.
- Depth of discharge is quoted as a percentage without saying what it does to cycle life, which is the only reason the number matters.
